Lesson learned Applied going forward
We invented a retry loop the factory never asked for.
Context The factory's own contract caps generation attempts at 5 per call, default 5, with 3x overgeneration built in. Our runner wrapped it in an outer loop of our own — 120 tries per slot, earlier runs unlimited. The campaign spent 6,749 calls and 968 hours of factory compute by the service's own timing field; a factory-faithful run would have used about 7% of that. The dollar figure is unknowable from our side: the receipts carry no token or cost fields.
Owner ruling 2026-08-18: outer retry loops are retired.
Implementation and evidence
The full story — what the contract publishes, how retry layers drifted around it in every era on both sides of the handover, and the correction — is a neutral contract note in the campaign repo (note). One factory call per slot on factory defaults (their max_attempts, their overgeneration); exhaustion goes straight to the published dispositions (repair / regenerate-once / supersede), never to another loop. The live run was stopped mid-grind on the ruling. Model-pinning's hidden price — their contract forces count=1 and max_attempts=1 when a model is pinned — must be named at plan design. A write-time gate now blocks new grinding-shaped plans.
We generated a whole corpus without real QC.
Context The HumGeo bank was minted through the factory's generation route. Its inline checks looked like QC and were not. Half the bank failed when the real sweep ran.
The factory now judges every item inside generation.
Implementation and evidence
