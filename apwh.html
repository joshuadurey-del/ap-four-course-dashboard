The 362-item batch and the paid sweep both start on the pool release, cautious 08-18 EOD KST. Earlier dates exist only as internal planning and are deliberately not published here, per the owner's 2026-08-17 cadence ruling that shared surfaces carry the cautious date.

What 08-21 assumes — read this before quoting the date. Everything after the pool opens is sequential: build 362 items, tag them, apply, materialize records, clean screen, then the single paid sweep. Measured legs: tagging runs 40 items/hour (three runs agreeing, 2 concurrent), so re-tagging the 362 regenerated items is 9.1 hours on its own; the paid sweep over 933 is roughly 2–3 hours at 6–8/chunk. Two legs are UNMEASURED and they are the ones that decide the date: (1) generation throughput for the 362 has no comparable prior run to rate; (2) the sweep pass rate is unknown because 0/933 verdicts have ever been spent — zero qc-record sidecars exist on disk. A meaningful sweep-fail share reopens the build and tag legs. 08-21 is therefore a clean-run-after-an-on-time-pool date, not a padded one; it is the cautious date on offer, but it carries no slack and should not be quoted as if it does.

Does 08-21 actually hold? Yes, and here is the arithmetic. Adding the per-line estimates below, the work that has to run in sequence after the pool opens comes to about 25 hours on a clean run and about 39 hours allowing for retries and a heavy failure loop, with the final walk unmeasured on top of both. From a cautious pool opening of 08-18 late evening, that finishes 08-20 in the morning at best and 08-20 in the afternoon at worst — inside 08-21 with roughly a day spare. So the date stands, and it was checked rather than defended. The dominant risk is not our speed, it is the pool opening date. If the pool slips a full day to 08-19 late evening, the same arithmetic lands 08-21 in the afternoon, at the wire with nothing left over. One more slip after that and 08-21 breaks. Two of the five legs below are also projections rather than measurements, and both sit on the slow side of the estimate if they are wrong.
